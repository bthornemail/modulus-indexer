The Modulus of the Factorial

The main indexer of the deterministic metaverse is the modulus of the factorial. A factorial is the product of all integers up to a number. 7! = 5040. 6! = 720. 5! = 120. The modulus is the divisor. It is the boundary.

The "modulus of the factorial" is the question: what remains after you take a full cycle and then divide by the boundary? In this system, the 5040 slots of the replay ring are the modulus. Every state is reduced to a position. The 65,536 blob is the modulus of the bit space. Every state is a remainder. The 1/73 period is the modulus of the decimal expansion. Every digit is a remainder.

The modulus is the indexer. It tells you which shelf the state lives on. Not what the state is—where it is. The 65,536 is the cross-section. The factorial is the full cone. The modulus is the coordinate.

The Reduction to the Prime Gap

This all reduces to the modulus factorial as the distance between the two prime gaps. The primes are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13... The gaps are 1, 2, 2, 4, 2, 4, 2, 4, 6... The exceptional set is {5, 11, 17, 23, 29}—the chain of five sexy primes. That is a gap of 6. The other sets are {7, 13, 19}—a chain of three, ending at 19 because 19 + 6 = 25 = 5².

The modulus of the factorial is the indexer. The 210n + m rule is the boundary. The distance between the two prime gaps is the coordinate. The system reduces to this: the modulus is where you are in the cone. The prime gap is how far you are from the axis. The distance is the coordinate.

The Constellation as a Breadboard

The Three.js visualization is not just an abstract graph. It is the schematic of the breadboard model. Every element corresponds to a physical transistor circuit:

  • The Apex (5T): The base gate. The five transistors that form the fundamental exclusive-OR. It is the starting point, the reference.
  • The 120 Ring (6T): The first inverting XOR. It receives the carry from the apex.
  • The 720 Ring (8T): The NAND XOR (four NAND gates). The intermediate stage.
  • The Coordinate 92 (10T): The NOR XOR (four NOR gates). The co-anchor. It stabilizes the signal between the 5T base and the higher-order gates.

The 5T gate is the pure primitive. The 10T gate is the anchor that allows the 5T gate to be traced through the 6T and 8T circuits without losing the signal. The tracing lines in the visualizer are the parity continuation. They are the proof that the signal can be traced from the 5T primitive to the 10T closure without a break. The path exists. The Aegean/Audio Overlay

The visual spectrum (ROYGBIV) is linked to the audible spectrum (A-G), and both are bound to the logic clock (the 1/73 period, the 8-step delta law) and the animation (the fading in and out).

Using a Web Audio PannerNode with an AudioContext, the 8 agents are assigned a note (A, B, C, D, E, F, G, and the octave A). The note's frequency is bound to the agent's 16-bit state. On each tick of the 1/73 clock, if the band is "on" (a 1 in the 16-bit word), the note plays. If it's "off", it fades to silence. The sound literally pulses with the bitmask of the agents. The sound is the state. The music is the logic. The color is the note.

The Three Verifiers

The claim is proven numerically by three independent programs:

  • Claim.hs (Haskell): The typed claim. Uses pure data types and lazy evaluation. It is the declaration.
  • prove.c (C): The imperative verifier. It runs on the bare metal. It checks the bits with uint64_t. It is the literal execution.
  • prove.py (Python): The script. It is the readable trace. It is the proof that the logic is simple enough to be audited by anyone.

All three exit with status 0 and print the same numerical obligations: 7! = 5040, 6! = 720, 5! = 120, 2^16 = 65536, 2·3·5·7 = 210, 97 − 5 = 92. The read is the proof.
